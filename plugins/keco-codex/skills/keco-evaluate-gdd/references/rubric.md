# GDD EDD Three-Dimension Rubric

## Scoring Method

Use `experience goal -> design response -> GDD evidence` for all dimensions. Give one aggregate score per dimension. The evaluation ranges guide evidence collection and must not become secondary scores.

| Dimension | Points | Evaluation range |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Experience Value | 30 | Target player, core fantasy, player motivation, differentiation, intended emotion, and experience pacing |
| Gameplay and Systems | 40 | Core loop, actions and choices, rules, feedback, difficulty, progression, economy, and balance |
| Content and Presentation | 30 | Content structure, levels, narrative, visual design, audio design, UI, information readability, and style consistency |

## Anchored Bands

| Quality band | Experience Value | Gameplay and Systems | Content and Presentation | Objective definition |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Severely missing | 0-9 | 0-12 | 0-9 | Critical design is absent or contradictory and cannot support the intended experience. |
| Partially established | 10-15 | 13-20 | Basic intent is visible, but major gaps prevent the dimension from being convincing. |
| Fundamentally sound | 16-21 | 21-28 | The main design works on paper, with explicit and locatable iteration gaps. |
| Clear and effective | 22-27 | 29-36 | The design is clear and coherent, with sufficient document evidence for the intended experience. |
| Near benchmark | 28-30 | 37-40 | Evidence is unusually complete and verifiable, with almost no material gaps. Use this band rarely. |

Choose a score within a band by comparing the breadth, specificity, consistency, and traceability of the evidence. Do not award points merely because a heading or design category exists.

## Evidence Attribution

1. Use only content explicitly stated in the current GDD revision. Treat unstated content as an evidence gap.
2. Assign the same evidence or root issue to one primary dimension. Never count it twice.
3. Assign UI that changes player action or decisions to Gameplay and Systems. Assign UI information hierarchy or presentation consistency to Content and Presentation.
4. Assign visual and audio direction to Content and Presentation. Score whether the documented direction supports the experience, not the quality of unbuilt assets.
5. Assign target players, fantasy, motivation, differentiation, emotional intent, and high-level pacing promises to Experience Value.
6. Keep document clarity, evidence coverage, ambiguity, and contradiction counts outside the score. Use them to set confidence.
7. Do not infer playability, feel, visual completion, audio quality, performance, stability, or player sentiment from a GDD.

## Confidence

- `high`: all three dimensions have specific, traceable evidence and no material ambiguity blocks attribution.
- `medium`: the score is supportable, but one or more dimensions contain notable gaps or ambiguities.
- `low`: substantial missing or contradictory content makes the numeric score highly provisional.

Confidence does not change the numeric total. List the source limitations that justify it.
